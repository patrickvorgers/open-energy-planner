How model selection works

The active forecast model is used now. Solar Automatic can activate machine learning after a successful historical forecast assessment. Separately captured daily forecasts compare available sources against the same measured production hours. PVWatts remains available when the required evidence is missing or the learned forecast does not improve accuracy. A manually selected source still needs usable data and compatible metering.

Household selection separately compares simulated planning costs. Better forecast accuracy does not guarantee a cheaper energy plan.

How forecasts and accuracy work

The latest forecasts observed before midnight are fixed for that calendar day. Intraday replanning never replaces them. Evaluation waits until the day has ended and all hourly measurements are available, including the ten-minute Recorder delay. Missing midnight forecasts and missing measurements stay unavailable. Summer and winter clock changes produce 23-hour and 25-hour days.

The daily error compares total predicted and measured kWh. The hourly bars also show timing differences that can cancel in the daily total. MAE, bias, RMSE and WAPE describe those hourly differences only after the whole day is eligible. Controlled-load timing changes and non-comparable PV boundaries do not receive an accuracy score. A zero measured total has no relative percentage error.

Forecast accuracy, simulated decision costs and actually measured savings are separate evidence. Solar source selection retains its own matched forward comparisons alongside this midnight graph. See Savings for measured savings.
